I am a PhD candidate in the Desplan lab at New York University. I study genetic programs that specify different kinds of neurons during development. Using the fly visual system as my model, I aim to learn how important regulators, including those implicated in spatial and temporal patterning, interact to define neuronal fates.

My work sits between developmental biology, neurobiology, and computational genomics. I use single-cell datasets, genetic tools, and comparative analysis to understand how neural diversity is produced, how cell identities are maintained, and how developmental logic can be read from large molecular atlases.

I also write occasional notes on computational biology, software setup, sequencing analysis, and the practical details that tend to matter when turning data into biological conclusions.
